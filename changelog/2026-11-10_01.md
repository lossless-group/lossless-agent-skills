## Why Care?

People new to building sites can't say what their site should say or look like,
and agents happily build anyway. These two skills put the deciding first, in a
form a beginner can actually do.

## What's New?

- **`write-site-narrative`** grills the person in the style of Matt Pocock's
  grill-me: one question at a time, each with a recommended answer, down a
  ten-branch tree (what it is, who it's for, their moment, the promise, proof,
  difference, feeling, never-say, next action, pages). It writes
  `context-v/narratives/<Site>-Narrative.md`, waits for sign-off, then develops
  the site's content as Markdown from it, with `TODO:` for missing facts and
  nothing invented.
- **`build-a-moodboard`** turns the narrative into 2 to 4 reference worlds,
  finds real images (Unsplash, museum open access, Wikimedia, Dribbble for
  reference only), checks each one loads, and writes a single-file board. The
  template keeps the board as a JSON block, with each world's heading lettering
  in the data, so anyone can edit it.
